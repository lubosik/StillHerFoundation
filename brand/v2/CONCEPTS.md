# Emblem v2, eleven concepts in two rounds

After the v1 monogram was rejected.

**Round two, the SHF monograms, is the current work and the selected mark is concept 8.**
Round one is the five non monogram concepts, kept below as submitted.

Her words: *"I don't like that the stem of the H goes through the S. It's giving money sign and
I don't like it. It's giving back vibes."*

Two things were wrong with v1, not one.

1. **The dollar read.** It was deliberate in v1 and it was a misreading of the earlier meeting.
   It is gone. No vertical stroke passes through an S anywhere in this set, in either round.
   What she objected to was that one stroke, so that one stroke is what was removed.
2. **It did not survive being small.** Screenshotted at 32px and 16px, v1 dissolves into a smudge.
   Its identity lived entirely in hairlines, and hairlines are the first thing a browser, an
   embroidery machine and a die maker throw away. Every mark below is built so its meaning lives
   in the outer silhouette, where it cannot be lost.

The benchmark she set is her friend's Crippled Cutie mark: a heart with a crystal bandaid over it.
The structure of that idea is worth naming, because it is what all eleven are trying to do.
It is **one tender object, plus one mark of injury rendered as something precious**. The bandaid
says hurt. The crystal says and it is beautiful anyway. One image, no explanation needed.

# Round two, the SHF monograms

Round one missed a requirement. She asked for SHF explicitly and more than once:

- *"when I do the still her foundation, I'm going to have the S.H.F. logo"*
- *"if we can make the SHF ... just have the SHF logo with the words written around it somehow"*
- *"you know how you have RR and it says at the bottom Rosen Relations? Same."*
- The Chanel reference she gave is the two C's, which is a monogram.

What she rejected in v1 was **one stroke**, not the idea of a monogram. *"I don't like that the
stem of the H goes through the S."* That is a complaint about a specific vertical producing a
dollar read. Round one read it as a rejection of monograms and dropped them entirely. That was
wrong, and this round puts SHF back.

## The direction, and what actually happened when I built it

The brief for this round was to make the SHF letters form the thyroid butterfly. The thyroid is
two lobes joined by a narrow isthmus, which is structurally three parts, so the proposal was
H as the body and the isthmus, S and F as the two lobes. I built it. Here is the honest account.

**The letters cannot be the wings.** The reason is specific and it is visible in the 32 and 16
columns of `preview.html`.

1. **S and F are not mirror images of each other.** A butterfly is mirror symmetric. An S and an F
   placed either side of an H produce a creature with one curved wing and one angular wing, which
   never resolves. That is concept 7, and it is submitted as a failure so the attempt is on record.
2. **An S is rotationally symmetric, not mirror symmetric.** Its two bulges sit on opposite
   corners, so one S can never make a same side wing pair. Two mirrored S's overlaid *do* produce
   four corner lobes, but their counters become two horizontal white ovals and the mark reads
   unmistakably as an owl or a cat face. I tested that at eight different sizes, rotations and
   widths, and as a union with no counters at all. It reads as a face every time.
3. **Splayed mirrored S's do work as a shape**, and that is concept 6. It is genuinely pretty at
   poster size. It dies below 64px, and it dies of exactly the disease that killed v1: a Didone
   puts its mass in the middle of a stroke and hairlines at the terminals, and a butterfly needs
   mass at the wing tips. There is no parameter setting that fixes this, because it is the
   letterform doing it, not the drawing.

**So the letters cannot be the wings. They can be in the wings.** That is concept 8 and it is the
resolution. Solid wings carry the mass and the silhouette. The S is cut clean out of the left wing,
the F out of the right, and the H stands between them as the body and the isthmus. The monogram
lives in the counters, which means it degrades in the one direction you want: as the mark gets
smaller the counters close and a butterfly is still there, instead of the whole thing turning to
smudge. At 256px you read SHF. At 16px you read a butterfly. Both are true and neither is a
compromise.

## 6. Letters as wings  ·  `concept-6-shf-wings-a.svg`

**The direct attempt.** An S and its mirror splayed as four wings, an H standing between them as
the body. Beautiful at poster size, gone by 64px. Submitted because the orchestrator asked to see
the attempts, not because it is usable.

## 7. S wing, F wing  ·  `concept-7-shf-wings-b.svg`

**The literal reading, and a documented failure.** S is the left wing, F is the right wing, H is
the body. It reads SHF perfectly well and it never reads as a butterfly, because the two letters
are not mirror images and the creature is therefore never symmetrical.

## 8. SHF Butterfly  ·  `concept-8-shf-butterfly.svg`  ·  **selected**

**The initials are the picture.** The thyroid butterfly with the S cut out of the left wing, the F
out of the right, and the H as the body and the isthmus, with a pearl set at the joint.

It carries everything at once. The monogram she asked for. The butterfly, which is the correct and
instantly readable symbol for the cause. The pearl, which is what a living body builds around an
irritant it cannot expel, and which is also the word we use for teeth. And it is legible from a
step and repeat down to a browser tab.

## 9. SHF Inline  ·  `concept-9-shf-inline.svg`

**The fashion house line.** Three letters on one line. The H and the F share a stem, and the
crossbar and the top arm run on past it. The S stands completely free. Nothing crosses it.

## 10. SHF Stacked  ·  `concept-10-shf-stacked.svg`

**The v1 arrangement with the offending stroke simply deleted.** The S on its own line above the
HF ligature, a gold hairline between them. Included because her objection was to one stroke, and
she may well find that removing it is all she wanted.

## 11. SHF Chanel  ·  `concept-11-shf-chanel.svg`

**The two C construction, done with the letter she actually has.** An S and its mirror close on an
HF ligature, so the mark is symmetrical and reads as an emblem rather than as a word. The doubled
letter is the device, exactly as the doubled C is at Chanel.

# What I picked, and why it changed

**Concept 8, the SHF Butterfly.** It replaces Her Brooch as the pick.

Her Brooch is still the better *drawing*. It is more elegant, the wing shape is more couture and
the pearl sits more naturally. But it has no letters in it, and SHF is a stated requirement, not a
preference. Concept 8 keeps everything Her Brooch had, the butterfly and the pearl and the
silhouette that survives being small, and adds the monogram. On the brief as actually written,
that is not a close call.

If she decides she does not want letters after all, **concept 1 is the fallback and nothing else
needs to change**, because the two marks share the same wing geometry, the same body position and
the same pearl.

## Honest read on what she may reject, round two

- **Concept 8's real risk is that the letters crowd the wings.** At 256px the S and the F take up
  most of the upper wings, so the butterfly is carried by the lower wings and the outline rather
  than by a big open wing shape. If she wants more butterfly and less monogram, the letters shrink
  and the wings open up, and the mark survives that edit.
- **Concept 6 will be liked and cannot be used.** It is the prettiest thing in round two at large
  size. Expect her to point at it. The answer has to be the 32px column, not an opinion.
- **Concept 11 may read as SHS rather than SHF.** The mirrored S is legible as an S, but the mark
  leads with symmetry and a viewer who does not already know the initials may not find the F.
- **Concepts 9 and 10 are correct and unexciting.** They are here so she has a plain option. Judged
  against a heart with a crystal bandaid, neither is a genius moment, and neither pretends to be.

## One production caveat on concept 8

The letter counters are fine in print and on screen at 40px and above. They are **not** fine as
stitched detail on a small patch: at a 40mm patch the S's thin strokes land near half a millimetre.
For embroidery, stamping and anything struck at small size, use the closed drawing, which is what
`favicon-v2.svg` already is. That is the same mark with the counters shut, not a different mark.

# Round one, the non monogram concepts

Kept as submitted, unchanged.

## 1. Her Brooch  ·  `concept-1-her-brooch.svg`  ·  round one's pick, now the fallback

**The thyroid is already butterfly shaped. Wear it as jewellery.**

The gland sits in the throat as two lobes joined by a narrow isthmus, which is why the butterfly is
the near universal symbol for thyroid disease. This draws that butterfly as a couture brooch rather
than a medical icon: swept wing, scalloped lower edge, a fine gold milgrain following the contour,
and a **pearl set in the body exactly where the isthmus sits**.

The pearl is the Crippled Cutie move. A pearl is what a living body builds around an irritant it
cannot expel, which is a precise description of an autoimmune disease. It is also the word we use
for teeth, which is her mother. The organ that betrayed her is set, polished and worn on the chest.

## 2. Pearl of Thirteen  ·  `concept-2-pearl-of-thirteen.svg`

**Thirteen teeth taken, thirteen pearls returned.**

One pearl in a heavy setting, ringed by thirteen more. Same pearl logic as above, carried alone and
counted. The count is the documentary: thirteen extractions on a promise that never came.

## 3. The Retied Ribbon  ·  `concept-3-the-retied-ribbon.svg`

**Take the awareness ribbon off the lapel and tie it properly.**

The folded loop everyone knows from a charity pin, rebuilt as a couture bow. Two loops, a knot, and
the two tails still crossing in the V of the ribbon it came from. It reads as solidarity and as
fashion at the same time, which is the safe space for women in business.

## 4. The Kept Promise  ·  `concept-4-the-kept-promise.svg`

**A seal is the physical object of a promise. Her mother was given one that was not kept.**

A wax seal with thirteen scallops and a bloom pressed into the face. The foundation puts its own
seal on the promise this time. It is also the only one of the five that already contains its own
ring, so it doubles as the step and repeat mark with no extra construction.

## 5. The Throat Locket  ·  `concept-5-the-throat-locket.svg`

**The locket hangs exactly where the thyroid sits.**

A heart locket on a fine chain. The illness and the mother are carried in the same place on the
body, and a locket is the one piece of jewellery whose entire purpose is to hold a person you have
lost. The idea is the best of the five. The drawing is the weakest, for reasons below.

## Round one's pick, for the record

**Concept 1, Her Brooch.** Within round one it is the only one of the five that is right on all four axes at once.

- **Concept.** It carries the disease, the mother and the luxury in one object, and none of the
  three needs a caption. The butterfly is correct for the cause and instantly readable. The pearl
  turns it from a medical symbol into something she would actually pin on a coat.
- **Legibility.** Verified, not assumed. Rendered at 16px in headless Chrome and inspected pixel by
  pixel, it still reads as a butterfly on cream, on white and on ink. Two of the other four do not.
- **Embroidery and stamping.** The whole mark is two solid wing shapes and one solid body column.
  Strip the gold hairline and the antennae and it stitches. Nothing in the silhouette depends on a
  line thinner than the body.
- **She asked for cute.** It is cute without being childish, and feminine without being a flower.

The pearl is why this is the pick rather than a stock thyroid butterfly. Without it the mark is a
correct but ordinary awareness icon. With it, the mark says the thing she actually says about
herself: the body did this, and she is still here, and she is not going to make it ugly.

## Honest read on what she may reject, round one

- **Concept 5, The Throat Locket, will be rejected and it deserves to be.** The idea is arguably
  the strongest in the set. The execution fails her own brief. At 32px the chain vanishes and at
  16px the whole thing is an undifferentiated blob. She asked specifically for a mark that works
  small. This one does not, and the screenshot in `preview.html` shows exactly that. It is included
  because the idea is worth a second attempt, not because this drawing is ready.
- **Concept 3, The Retied Ribbon, is the likeliest to be called generic.** A teal bow in 2026 reads
  as a fashion account before it reads as a foundation. It also risks the same fate as the high
  heel she already ruled out, which is being mistaken for a beauty brand. It is genuinely cute, and
  it is the weakest at saying what the organisation does.
- **Concept 4, The Kept Promise, risks reading as a spa or a jam label.** A scalloped teal disc with
  a flower in it is a well worn shape. It survives every size test, which is why it is here, but the
  bloom is the least specific idea in the set. If she likes the seal shape, the honest fix is to
  press concept 1 into the wax rather than a flower.
- **Concept 2, Pearl of Thirteen, is the one she may love for the story and cool on for the image.**
  Told out loud it is the best line in the set. Looked at cold, a ring of beads is close to a
  generic halo, and at 16px the thirteen pearls fuse into a single circle, so the count, which is
  the whole point, is invisible at the size it is most often seen.
- **Concept 1, the pick, has one real risk.** The thyroid butterfly is used by a lot of thyroid
  charities, so it is not an ownable shape on its own. What makes it hers is the pearl, the milgrain
  and the scalloped lower wing. If she wants more distance from the category, the move is to push
  the brooch further, not to abandon the butterfly.

## Deliberately not submitted

- **A woman's silhouette, water, a high heel, and foundations as in a building.** Ruled out by her,
  and she is right about all four.
- **A keystone.** It is a beautiful idea for a woman holding up her own ecosystem, and it is the
  top of the arch rather than the bottom, so it escapes the word foundation. It does not escape the
  *look*. Drawn as an emblem it is a trapezoid, and a trapezoid reads as construction or as a tech
  logo, which lands in exactly the territory she rejected.
- **A mirror, a laurel and a crown.** All three are legible and all three are decoration, not an
  argument. None of them says anything specific about Graves, about her mother, or about her.

## The one colour rule for the mark

This is a rule, not a preference, and it exists because it has already gone wrong once.

In v1, `public/brand/emblem.svg` was drawn in `--teal` `#5BBCC9` and `public/favicon.svg` was drawn
in `--teal-ink` `#1F6670`. The same mark, in two different hues. That is what she saw when she said
the emblem looked darker than the rest of the page, and it is what she meant by *"I need the colour
address to be the same."*

**The rule.** Every file in the v2 family resolves to the same colour identity:

| Role | Token | Hex | Where it is used in the mark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The mark itself | `--teal` | `#5BBCC9` | The wings. The large fill. This is the colour of the logo. |
| Structure inside the mark | `--teal-ink` | `#1F6670` | The body, the antennae, and all lockup type. |
| Hairline only | `--gold` | `#C9A961` | Milgrain, the seal rings, the rule in the stacked lockup. Never a fill. |
| The pearl | `--white` | `#FFFFFF` | The set stone only. |

Three things follow from it.

1. **The favicon may be simplified. It may not be recoloured.** `favicon-v2.svg` is the same
   butterfly, the same pearl and the same H in the same two teals, drawn larger and heavier
   inside the box, with the letter counters closed. The H still reads at 32px. Where it needs
   more presence at 16px it takes it from mass and stroke width, never from swapping to the
   darker teal. `emblem-v2.svg` and `favicon-v2.svg` contain exactly the same three hexes.
2. **No new hexes.** Every value above is copied from `src/styles/tokens.css`. There are no custom,
   nudged or darkened variants anywhere in the v2 files. All seven files have been audited and
   contain only these four hexes plus `--cream` `#F7F2E9` where a shape is knocked out of a fill.
3. **If a future mono version is needed**, it is made by dropping colours out of this set, never by
   substituting a new one. Teal on cream, or white over photography. Not a third teal.

## How this was checked

Playwright would not install on this machine, so every screenshot was taken with headless Chrome
directly, at `--force-device-scale-factor=1` so that a 16px mark is 16 real device pixels and not a
downscaled 32. Each 16px render was then upscaled with nearest neighbour interpolation and looked
at, pixel by pixel, rather than judged from the code. `brand/v2/preview.html` is the contact sheet:
all eleven concepts at 256, 64, 32 and 16, on cream and on ink, plus the four production files.
Round two went through three rounds of parameter exploration before concept 8, comparing eight
wing geometries, five body treatments and two favicon bodies on screen rather than on reasoning.
