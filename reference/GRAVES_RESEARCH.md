## The precision that matters most

**Graves' disease is a cause of hyperthyroidism, not another name for it.** Hyperthyroidism has several causes. The NHS lists Graves' disease, thyroid nodules, and some medicines such as amiodarone. Graves' is the most common cause. About 4 in 5 people with an overactive thyroid have Graves' disease (NHS, and NIDDK gives the same proportion for the United States).

## Where sources disagree, and what the page does about it

**How often thyroid eye disease occurs.** NHS "around 1 in 4". Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Trust "about 25%". ATA "a third". Cleveland Clinic "about 1 in 3". NIDDK "more than 1 in 3". BTF hyperthyroidism leaflet "about one third". BTF eye disease leaflet "about 40%". MedlinePlus Genetics "between 25 and 50 percent".
The page publishes the range and says sources differ, rather than picking one and presenting it as settled.

**Prevalence.** NIDDK "nearly 1 in 100 Americans". MedlinePlus Genetics "about 1 in 200 people". Both are NIH or NLM sources. No UK figure was found on a page that loaded. The page gives both, attributed.

**Female to male ratio.** ATA "7 to 8 times more common in women than men" for Graves' specifically. The NHS "10 times" figure is for overactive thyroid in general, not Graves', so the page uses the ATA figure.

**Age of onset.** NIDDK "more common in people older than 30". Endotext "30 to 50". The NHS "20 to 40" is again for overactive thyroid in general.

**BTF opening hours.** Two BTF pages list different weekdays for the same 11am to 3pm line. The page publishes the number and tells people to check the website for current days.

**Stress as a trigger.** BTF and Cleveland Clinic mention it, hedged. NHS and NIDDK do not. The page uses hedged wording.

## Do not publish. Each of these was seen and deliberately excluded.

1. Anything attributed to Mayo Clinic. The pages returned 403 and were never read.
2. Anything attributed to NICE NG145. Same. The only NICE content seen was a search snippet.
3. MedlinePlus encyclopedia article 000358. Connection reset three times, never read.
4. "Graves described three women in 1835" and "the Merseburg triad". From search snippets, not from a page that was read. The page says "about 150 years ago", which is the ATA's own wording.
5. A single bare prevalence figure. Two NIH sources disagree, so both are given with attribution.
6. "10 times more common in women." That NHS figure is for overactive thyroid generally, not Graves'.
7. "Typically between 20 and 40." Same problem.
8. "About 20% develop eye disease." Search snippet only.
9. Increased thyroid cancer risk from untreated Graves'. Cleveland Clinic only, not corroborated by NHS, NIDDK or ATA.
10. Selenium and vitamin D deficiency as risk factors. Cleveland Clinic only.
11. "Estrogen as your main sex hormone" as a risk factor. The plainer NHS and NIDDK wording is used instead.
12. Personal phone numbers of BTF volunteer telephone contacts. They belong to named individuals and change. The page links to the BTF page instead.
13. Thyroid UK mobile number 07305 568010. Appears on their media and PR page and is likely for press.
14. Any statement that Thyroid UK runs a telephone helpline. Their own support page says they have no telephone Support Networkers. Their number is an office line.
15. Dosages, treatment durations framed as advice, or "first line" language. The page describes options and points to a doctor.
16. Any wording implying Graves' and hyperthyroidism are the same thing.
